Rubbing moves electrons

Every material grips its electrons with a different strength. When two rub, the one lower in the triboelectric series takes electrons from the other. Nothing is created: the balloon's charge always matches the cloth's, with the opposite sign.

A neutral wall still pulls

The wall's electrons can't leave their atoms, but they can lean. Near a negative balloon they shift back a little, so the wall's surface turns slightly positive. That nearby positive layer wins and holds the balloon.

Metal lets charge flow

In the metal ball electrons move freely, so a charged balloon drives them to or from ground. Close enough, the field rips the air open and a spark carries charge across. Rubber is an insulator, so only the charge near the spark leaves.

Damp air drains charge

A thin film of water on surfaces gives charge a slow path to leak away. Turn the humidity up and charge fades within seconds. That is why static shocks peak in dry winter air.
